### [P1] Payment void can leave invoice-wide refunds above the refundable excess
- Category: material risk (money correctness)
- Rule: MON-04, MON-07; PAY-01
- Evidence: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-core-sales-design.md:862–864` defines a cap across the invoice's refundable excess and a per-payment cap. Lines 872 and 878 refuse a payment void only when a non-voided refund is against that same payment; refunds against other payments remain. Line 420 says voided payments no longer count in refund caps. Changes in v4.7 item 1(c) leaves this case open, and Verification (r) at line 1104 does not cover it.
- Failure scenario: on a $100 invoice, payments of $100 and $50 are recorded and a valid $50 refund is recorded against the second payment. Voiding the first payment is allowed because it has no refund, leaving $50 in non-voided payments and $50 in refunds, while the invoice owes $100. The existing refund was valid when recorded, but now exceeds the invoice's refundable excess of zero.
- Why it matters: the void changes the money state used by the invoice-wide refund cap without a rule for already recorded refunds or a recovery path.
- Required correction: Charlie must decide what happens when a payment void leaves refunds on other payments above the recalculated refundable excess; specify the server-side guard or correction rule and add a forced concurrency/property case. Do not implement the open branch as an assumed policy.
- Owner: owner (policy), then builder (spec and test)
- Verification: exercise the example above and the concurrent refund/void orders; prove refund and net-paid totals follow the selected policy.
